Motivation

Both vendors launched cheaper flagships within hours on September 22, 2026 — GPT-6 Sol and Luna from OpenAI, Claude Opus 5.5 from Anthropic — a story the journal's daily digest format cannot serve as an evergreen landing page. Date-rot makes digests poor targets for forming comparison queries, so a verdict-first evergreen page in the automation cluster captures the durable Sol-versus-Luna-versus-Opus demand and strengthens the internal links around the Astra launch piece and the prices and benchmarks pages.

What changed

One new comparison joined the journal at cheap flagship models: Sol vs Luna vs Opus 5.5, with its distribution pack for social and feed syndication. The piece mirrors the Jev comparison structure: when-cheaper-beats-flagship framing, a head-to-head grid with every figure vendor-cited or marked unverified, per-model cards with price, speed, context and best-fit, a self-host-versus-API table, and verify-on-your-workload caveats. Outbound links cover both vendor announcements, both vendor pricing pages, and the independent first-impression and news-cycle sources; inward links point at the prices page, the benchmarks page, and the Astra launch analysis.

How it was done

The guide was drafted from the existing post scaffold with no template or navigation changes, keeping the content-only scope the issue set. No invented numbers: prices, cached-input rates, speed claims, and benchmark rows each cite the vendor announcement or the independent source beside the claim, and context windows plus absolute Sol/Luna latency are marked unverified where the launch materials stay silent. The deterministic site build regenerated the journal indexes, the topic hub, the feeds, the sitemap, the search index, and the per-post share card; the test suite and the secret scan ran on the branch head before the pull request opened.
