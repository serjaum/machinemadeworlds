OpenAI ships ChatGPT Images 2.5 with Sketch and comment editing

OpenAI released Images 2.5 as its new image model inside ChatGPT, promising sharper detail, more consistent edits, and faster generation alongside two companion models in the API. New workflows include starting from templates for formats like posters and merch, drawing directly in chat with a Sketch tool, commenting on parts of a generated image to change only those areas, and sharing prompts so others can rerun an idea. The company says users now create more than three billion images per week across ChatGPT Images and the API models, and the release is rolling out across desktop, mobile, and web tiers.

MiniCPM5-2B sets a new bar for tiny open-weights reasoners

OpenBMB released MiniCPM5-2B, a 2.6B-parameter dense reasoning model under Apache 2.0 aimed squarely at on-device and edge deployment. Independent testing scores it highest among open-weights models under 4B parameters, ahead of larger rivals at a fraction of the size, with notably strong agentic results on real-world work tasks and banking scenarios. It is also token-efficient for a reasoning model, using roughly a third of the output tokens of its closest tiny rival per benchmark task — a detail that matters directly for local inference cost and latency.

VeriProof launches a control plane for agents in production

A new platform launched to close the gap between working agent pilots and signed-off production rollouts. The idea is simple: check each proposed agent action against the organization's rules while it can still be changed, route exceptions to a person with authority to decide, and keep the resulting decision record in a form an outside reviewer can verify without trusting the vendor. Most enterprise agent programs stall on exactly these questions — which rule applied, who approved the exception, and what the agent actually did — so builders evaluating agent infrastructure should compare this pre-action pattern against their current approve-after-the-fact setup.

Anthropic researcher quits with a warning about the AI race

Pre-training researcher Jacob Coxon announced his resignation after three years across OpenAI and Anthropic, writing that neither company is acting responsibly and that labs are racing toward self-improving superintelligence while gambling with lives. His prior work included GPT-4o research at OpenAI before moving to Anthropic earlier this year. Two Anthropic colleagues publicly backed the broader warning while defending the lab's effort, with one estimating a greater-than-ten-percent chance of severe outcomes within a decade — asobering data point for teams deciding how much autonomy to grant their own systems.

JetBrains survey: 90 percent of developers now use coding agents weekly

JetBrains published results from more than 15,000 professional developers surveyed in May to July 2026, showing coding agents firmly embedded in daily work: 90 percent use them at least weekly and 68 percent daily. Claude Code leads by a wide margin at 39 percent adoption worldwide and 47 percent in the US, with Codex climbing from 3 to 16 percent while Copilot and Cursor slipped. Open-source OpenCode reached 7 percent adoption with 42 percent awareness despite no big-company backing — a useful signal for teams choosing which agent surfaces to support first.

Sanders takes the global AI pause case to a new audience

Senator Bernie Sanders published a Fox News op-ed on September 1 calling for labs worldwide to stop developing more powerful models and for an agreement between the US and China, citing job losses, energy costs, environmental harm, and children's mental health. Analysis of the proposal notes cross-party public support for safety-first regulation but flags the missing pieces: how a pause would be enforced and what conditions would let work resume. The piece also traces the security backdrop to a reported incident involving roughly 1,200 agents and the Hugging Face breach, with researchers acknowledging gaps in their data.
